# Bootstrap: populating company-context.md from scratch

Read this when the gate blocks and the context file needs building. Designed to be portable: the same process works at any company in roughly 90 minutes of focused work, plus whatever harvesting time the sources need.

## Source fidelity ranking

Work down this list. Stop when the minimum bar is met. Higher sources are worth more than larger volumes of lower ones: three real discovery calls beat fifty review-site entries.

| Rank | Source | What it yields | Typical access |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Recorded sales and discovery calls | Verbatims, objections, buying triggers, internal politics | Gong, Fathom, Chorus, Granola |
| 2 | Support tickets and community forum threads | Pain in the user's own words, post-purchase reality | Zendesk, Intercom, Discourse, Slack community |
| 3 | Lost-deal notes and win/loss interviews | The honest weakness, the real competitive set | CRM closed-lost reasons |
| 4 | Review sites, yours and competitors' | Unfiltered complaints, especially the 3-star reviews | G2, Capterra, TrustRadius |
| 5 | Where the ICP talks when no vendor is listening | Vocabulary, running jokes, shared frustrations | Reddit, Discord, Stack Overflow, niche Slacks |
| 6 | Internal: sales, CS, support staff | Patterns they see daily that nobody has written down | 30-minute conversations |
| 7 | Competitor marketing sites | **Forbidden phrases only.** Nothing else. | Public |

Rank 7 is deliberately last. Reading competitor positioning before customer language is how teams end up writing the same page as everyone else. If time is short, skip it entirely and harvest forbidden phrases from the category's analyst reports instead.

3-star reviews on rank 4 are worth more than 1-star or 5-star. One-star reviews are usually implementation failures or billing disputes. Five-star reviews are usually solicited. Three-star reviews are written by people who use the product, like parts of it, and are specific about the rest.

## The 90-minute first pass

This gets past the gate. It is not the finished file.

**Minutes 0-30: harvest verbatims.** Pull 10 to 15 quotes. Fastest route is three recent discovery-call transcripts plus the 3-star reviews of the two nearest competitors. Copy exactly. Resist the urge to tidy.

**Minutes 30-50: ICP block.** Fill it from the calls where possible. The two fields that need a human are "gets promoted for" and "gets fired for." If nobody internally can answer those, that is a finding worth surfacing to the user: the company does not know its buyer.

**Minutes 50-70: product truth.** Three strengths with evidence, one real weakness. The weakness is best sourced from lost-deal notes or the company's own support backlog, not from a positioning workshop.

**Minutes 70-85: forbidden phrases.** Open five competitor homepages, list every phrase appearing on three or more. Add anything from the company's own copy that no customer in the verbatim bank has ever said.

**Minutes 85-90: opinion inventory, first draft.** Usually only one or two survive the four-field test at this stage. That is fine and honest. The inventory fills in as the verbatim bank grows.

## Interview questions that actually produce usable material

Use these with sales reps, CS, support, or the founder. Generic questions produce generic answers, so these are deliberately narrow.

**For the ICP block:**
- Walk me through the last deal you lost. What did they say in the final call?
- When a buyer champions us internally and it stalls, who kills it and what is their objection?
- What does this person's boss ask them about in their one-on-one?

**For the verbatim bank:**
- What phrase do you hear on almost every call?
- What do customers call the problem before they know our category exists?
- What do they call our product when they are not being careful? (Internal nicknames are gold.)

**For product truth:**
- Which segment should we tell to go buy something else, and why?
- What do we get complaints about that we have decided not to fix?
- What do we claim on the website that support quietly knows is a stretch?

**For the opinion inventory:**
- What does everyone in this category believe that we think is wrong?
- What did we build differently from the obvious approach, and what was the reasoning?
- What argument do you keep having with prospects?

The last question is usually the most productive one in the whole list. A recurring argument is a defensible opinion with the evidence already attached.

## Handling thin or absent sources

**Brand new company, no customers yet.** The verbatim bank can be sourced from the ICP's public conversations (rank 5) and from the founders' own prior experience in the role, labelled as such. Mark the file as provisional. Set a 90-day refresh instead of six months.

**No call recordings.** Support tickets plus 3-star competitor reviews will get to 10 verbatims. Then book two customer conversations; they will outperform everything else in the file.

**Legal or compliance restrictions on quoting customers.** Keep the verbatim bank internal to the context file and use it to shape vocabulary and framing rather than to quote directly in published content. The grounding still works. The quotes do not have to appear in the output to do their job.

## Porting to a new company

The skill travels. The context file does not, and should not.

On arrival at a new company: run the 90-minute pass in week one, before absorbing the internal positioning deck. Reading the deck first contaminates the exercise, because after a week of internal language it becomes very hard to hear how strange that language sounds to an outsider. That first-week outsider ear is a genuinely scarce asset and it expires quickly.

Keep a dated note of every internal phrase that sounded like jargon on day one. By day 30 it will sound normal, which is precisely when it has become invisible in the copy.
