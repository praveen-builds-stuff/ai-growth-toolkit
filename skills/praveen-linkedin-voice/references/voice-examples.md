# Praveen Visal — Voice Examples

Annotated reference posts for calibrating his writing style.

---

## Post 1: The Friday Log-Off Post
*Topic: work-life, personal productivity*
*Tone: curious, warm, slightly playful*

---

> What does logging off at 3 PM on every Friday look like?🤔
>
> I had a chance to listen to Rohan, Chief Customer Officer and Arjun, Head of Freshworks HR Cloud - Freshteam, have a casual conversation on why we should have our own 'think' hours.
>
> (No, I didn't eavesdrop. It was a Zoom session.)
>
> My eyebrows went up when Rohan said he never works post 3 PM on Fridays.
>
> Here's why he feels we should try that too.
>
> 👉 Your wonderful weekend starts early.
> 👉 After 3 PM is when you should log off from work and take time to think. Think and find what you enjoyed doing in that week and try doing more of that in the coming week.
>
> THINK. PLAN. WORK. REPEAT.
>
> This way, you'll understand what you're good at, fall in love with what you do, and eventually be more productive.
>
> Food for thought - gobbled!😊

---

### What's working in this post

**"(No, I didn't eavesdrop. It was a Zoom session.)"**
This is his best line. Pure Praveen. Self-aware, breaks the fourth wall, gets a smile without trying too hard. Preserve this energy in every post.

**"My eyebrows went up when..."**
Physical, embodied reaction. Not "I was surprised" — his actual face did something. This is specific and human.

**"Food for thought - gobbled!"**
Cheeky, unexpected, doesn't overstay its welcome. A good Praveen sign-off doesn't explain itself.

**Opens with a question that creates a visual**
"What does logging off at 3 PM on every Friday look like?" — you immediately picture it. Strong hook.

**Names people with roles**
"Rohan, Chief Customer Officer and Arjun, Head of Freshworks HR Cloud" — gives the reader context before they care. Do this every time.

### What could be stronger

The bullet points ("👉 Your wonderful weekend starts early") soften what could be a sharper insight. The THINK. PLAN. WORK. REPEAT. line is trying to be punchy but reads more like a motivational poster.

The ending explains too much ("you'll understand what you're good at, fall in love with what you do, eventually be more productive") — could end at REPEAT and leave more air.

## Post 2: The Webinar Post
*Topic: event promotion for Testsigma's masterclass*
*Tone: irreverent, self-aware, promotional*

---

> Webinars are boring. They are just sales decks being presented on a video call with a poll at the climax that asks "How many of you will attend our next (boring) webinar?"
>
> But not all of them. This one webinar series we've been hosting at Testsigma has recently been gaining traction in the QA community. Attendees emailed us back with great feedback while asking for recordings to share with their team - so it should probably be worth it, isn't it?
>
> The good part is it hasn't ended. The final session of "Context to Execution" Masterclass series is happening tomorrow evening at 7.30 PM IST.
>
> The best part is we're bringing Meera Rao and James Whitfield as speakers for a fire side chat on AI in testing, the future of QA, and how teams are leveraging AI to match the speed of software development.
>
> If you're in QA and trying to learn how your role is evolving today, you'll love watching them live. Register now: [link]

---

### What's working in this post

**"Webinars are boring."**
Two words. Stops the scroll. No hedging, no context. Perfect opening.

**"...a poll at the climax that asks 'How many of you will attend our next (boring) webinar?'"**
The parenthetical "(boring)" inside the quoted poll is sharp. He's doing the work of the joke without over-explaining it.

**The self-aware framing**
He's the marketer at a company calling his own industry's content boring. That's a credibility move, not just a hook. Keep this energy — he's always allowed to be the insider critic.

### What needs to be stronger (do not repeat these patterns)

**"But not all of them."** — Too fast. One sentence of bold claim, then immediate retreat. Hold the tension.

**"gaining traction"** — Marketing-speak. Replace with something real.

**"it should probably be worth it, isn't it?"** — Double hedge kills the momentum. Never do this.

**"fire side chat on AI in testing, the future of QA, and how teams are leveraging AI to match the speed of software development"** — Generic. What do Laveena and Richard actually *believe* that others don't? That's the hook.

**"Register now"** — Weakest possible CTA. What will someone walk away knowing?

---

## Distilled Voice Rules (from both posts)

1. First sentence: bold, visual, or counterintuitive. No warm-up.
2. Parentheticals are a superpower. Use them for humor and self-awareness.
3. Name people with context. Always.
4. Physical reactions ("my eyebrows went up") beat abstract reactions ("I was surprised").
5. Sign-offs can be playful. They don't have to be CTAs.
6. Never double-hedge. Pick a lane.
7. Marketing-speak is the enemy. Read every draft aloud and kill anything that sounds like a press release.
8. End with something the reader chews on — not something that closes the loop.
